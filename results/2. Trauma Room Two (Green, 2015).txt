  Phase 2: Conceptual Analysis

  Wilson's Four Methods

  Purposive Method — The author's intent is to take the reader "inside the hidden emotional landscape of emergency medicine" and reveal "the profound moments
  that often occur in emergency rooms." Purpose: to illuminate the human side of ER work through narrative.

  Figure-Ground Method — The central figure is emergency medicine as lived human experience — not clinical/technical medicine, but the emotional and
  existential dimensions of working in and passing through an emergency room. The trauma room itself is the unifying setting.

  Objective Method — Key recurring concepts: emergency rooms/departments, trauma, patients, families, staff, life and death, emotional experience. "Status
  epilepticus" in the TOC is the only clinical term; the rest are universal human themes.

  Cohesion Method — What unifies the 16 stories is the emergency room setting and the emotional/human moments that occur there. The rule of selection: each
  story captures a profound moment from the author-physician's ER experience.
