Abstract: "Since its first publication Isabel Rivers' sourcebook has established itself as the essential student guide to English Renaissance Poetry. It       
provides an account of the main classical and Christian ideas, outlining their meaning, their origins and their transmission to the Renaissance; illustrates   
the ways in which Renaissance poetry drew on classical and Christian ideas; contains extracts from key classical and Christian texts and relates these to the  
extracts of the English poems which draw on them; and includes suggestions for further reading, and an invaluable bibliographical appendix. In this new        
edition, the bibliographies have been updated to take account of important recent publications in philosophy, religion and Renaissance studies."--Jacket.\

  Phase 2: Analytical Methods                                                                                                                                  
                                                                  
  Purposive Method: Author's intent is to help students understand the intellectual background — classical philosophy and Christian theology — that English    
  Renaissance poets drew upon, and to illustrate those connections with paired textual extracts.
                                                                                                                                                               
  Figure-Ground Method: The central figure is the relationship between classical/Christian intellectual traditions and English Renaissance poetry — not the    
  poetry itself in isolation, nor the philosophy/theology in isolation, but the transmission of ideas into poetic practice.
                                                                                                                                                               
  Objective Method: Dominant recurring concepts: English Renaissance poetry, classical ideas (Platonism, Neoplatonism, Stoicism), Christian ideas (Reformation,
   Protestant theology, biblical typology), and the transmission/influence relationship between source traditions and poetry.

  Cohesion Method: The selection principle is intellectual traditions that demonstrably shaped English Renaissance poetry. Each chapter isolates one tradition 
  and traces its path into the poetry. Excluded: traditions without clear poetic influence; non-English poetry; post-Renaissance reception.

  Phase 4: Aboutness Statement

  ▎ This work of literary criticism and intellectual history examines how classical philosophical traditions (Platonism, Neoplatonism, Stoicism, cosmology) and
   Christian theological ideas (Reformation, Protestant theology, biblical exegesis and typology) influenced English Renaissance poetry. Organized by 
  intellectual tradition rather than by poet, it is a student sourcebook that pairs extracts from classical and Christian source texts with the English poems  
  that drew upon them. Bridging topics include humanism, allegory, and numerology. Intended for students at an introductory to intermediate level.
