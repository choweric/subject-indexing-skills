  Phase 2: Wilson's Four Methods

  Purposive Method (Author's Intent):
  The author aims to show that indigenous rituals in Toracari generate cultural models that reproduce deference to the state and landlords — countering narratives of
   structural social transformation under Bolivia's plurinational reforms. The irony highlighted is that the state adopted indigenous rituals as its legitimating
  language while those same rituals reinforce asymmetrical relations.

  Figure-Ground Method (Central Figure):
  The central figure is ritual as a mechanism of cultural reproduction — specifically, how Andean rituals produce and maintain deference to authority (state,
  landlords). The ground against which this stands is Bolivia's plurinational state reforms and indigenous politics.

  Objective Method (Frequency of Concepts):

  relations, indigeneity politics) serves to demonstrate how cultural continuity persists through ritual despite proclaimed state transformation.

  Phase 4: Aboutness Statement

  ▎ This scholarly ethnography, grounded in years of fieldwork in the Toracari valley of Bolivia, examines how Andean indigenous rituals produce cultural models of
  deference to the state and landlords, challenging claims that Bolivia's plurinational reforms have brought structural social transformation. The work analyzes
  specific rituals (Llanthu, Kumbre), campesino disengagement from the state, mining conflicts including the nationalization of a Canadian mining company, the
  politics of indigeneity, and the historical roots of landlord authority. It contributes to anthropological debates on ritual effectiveness, cultural reproduction,
  and indigenous politics in Latin America.
